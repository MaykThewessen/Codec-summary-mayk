Which codec wins,
and exactly where

Every image codec argument collapses into two questions: how much of the picture are you willing to throw away, and how many decoders do you need to survive. Position on those two axes decides the winner. Everything else is detail.

The HEIC row is the whole problem in one line. The slider question

Where the knee actually sits

The instinct that there is a sweet spot around 60 to 80 is correct, and the data puts a sharper edge on it. Below the knee, bytes buy visible quality. Above it, bytes buy almost nothing: the file keeps growing to encode noise you cannot see.

The HDR lane

Ten bits, and the Lightroom problem

Why HEIC exists

HEIF is a container; on an iPhone or a recent Canon it holds HEVC-coded 10-bit image data. Ten bits is the point: 8-bit JPEG cannot carry an HDR tone range without banding the sky. The camera writes HEIC because the same silicon already encodes HEVC video.

Why it stops there

HEVC carries patent licensing, which is precisely why Chrome and Firefox never shipped it and why Windows charges for the decoder. AVIF (AV1) and JPEG XL were designed around that problem and are royalty-free. They also do 10-bit and 12-bit, so nothing is lost by leaving HEIC at the capture stage.

The export path that works

Lightroom will not write HEIC, and it does not need to. Its HDR output writes AVIF, JPEG XL, and gain-map JPEG. Gain-map JPEG is the interesting one: HDR displays read the map, every other decoder sees an ordinary JPEG, so it degrades gracefully instead of failing.

What to keep

Keep the raw file as the master, always. If you want a rendered master too, JPEG XL lossless from the 16-bit render is the strongest option on the page. Keep HEIC only as what came off the camera.

Corrections and confirmations

Received wisdom, checked against the measurements

Holds up
"There is an optimum JPEG quality around 60 to 80."

Holds up
"A better JPEG encoder already helps a lot."

The strongest cost-benefit move available, because nothing downstream has to change: the output is still a JPEG that every decoder since 1992 reads. mozjpeg's trellis quantisation and jpegli's improved quantisation matrices both close a meaningful part of the gap to WebP without touching your delivery stack. This page's JPEG numbers come from stock libjpeg-turbo, so they are the pessimistic case; a better encoder shifts the JPEG curve up and to the left.

Right, with one correction
"WebP is mainly much better for screenshots."

Half right
"PNG is too large, but lossy PNG is a good and little-known improvement."

True, and it is a trap
"HEIF is the format I like most."

As a capture format the judgement is sound: 10-bit, HDR, half the size of the JPEG the same camera would write. The trap is that liking it does not make it publishable. It is missing from two of three browser engines, it costs money to decode on Windows, and your editor will not export it. Everything you like about HEIC is also true of AVIF and JPEG XL, which are royalty-free and which Lightroom does export. The affection is better directed one format over.

Worth reconsidering
"PNG has a large file size, so I do not like it much."

PNG is not competing in the same event. It is an authoring and interchange format: exact pixels, alpha, and a decoder in literally everything. Judged as a delivery format it is expensive; judged as the thing you hand to another tool, it is correct and its size is irrelevant. Keep it for masters and intermediates, and convert at publish time.

Operational

What to actually type

Settings below are the ones that landed on a high-quality target on this corpus, not encoder defaults.

Photographs, delivered on the web

# the modern pair, both royalty-free, both in every current browser
avifenc -q  -s 4 photo.png photo.avif   # smaller below visually-lossless
cjxl photo.png photo.jxl -d 1.0 -e 7    # smaller above it, and does lossless

# the fallback everything can read. use a modern encoder, not stock libjpeg.
cjpegli -q  photo.png photo.jpg
mozjpeg -quality  -progressive photo.png > photo.jpg

# already have a JPEG you cannot re-encode? strip and re-pack it losslessly.
jpegtran -copy none -progressive -optimize old.jpg > new.jpg

Screenshots, UI captures, diagrams, anything with text in it

# exact pixels, smallest file, universal support: the default answer
cwebp -lossless -z 9 -q 100 shot.png -o shot.webp

# if the output must stay a .png
oxipng -o 4 --strip safe shot.png          # lossless, typically 5-15% off
pngquant --quality=70-90 --speed 1 shot.png # palette, much larger saving

# never do this to a screenshot
cjpeg -quality 80 shot.png                  # bigger than the PNG and visibly worse

HDR and 10-bit

# camera writes HEIC. transcode before publishing, keep the raw as master.
avifenc --depth 10 --yuv 444 -q 63 -s 4 shot.heic shot.avif
cjxl shot.heic shot.jxl -d 1.0 -e 7

Method

How these numbers were produced, and where not to trust them

Quality was scored with SSIMULACRA2, which correlates far better with human judgement than PSNR or SSIM and does not reward the blurring that older metrics reward. Each codec was swept across its own quality ladder; the rate at a given perceptual target was found by interpolating that codec's own curve per image, then taking the geometric mean across images.

The JPEG figures are pessimistic

Encoding went through stock libjpeg-turbo. mozjpeg and jpegli both do considerably better at the same quality setting, so the real-world gap between a well-encoded JPEG and the modern codecs is narrower than plotted here.

The photographs are small

The reference set is 768 by 512. Modern camera output is 20 to 50 times larger, and the newer codecs generally do relatively better at higher resolution, so their lead is if anything understated.

One synthetic image is one data point

The flat-graphics conclusions come from a single rendered UI. The effect is large enough that the direction is safe, but do not read the exact percentages as general constants.

AVIF lossless is missing

The Python binding used here could not produce bit-exact AVIF: its lossless flag still round-trips through a lossy colour conversion. AVIF lossless is not competitive on flat graphics anyway, but the number is absent rather than measured.

Scripts and raw measurements live in the repository beside this page, so every figure can be regenerated or argued with.
